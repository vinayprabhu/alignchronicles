On June 24, 2020, Abeba Birhane and I released our paper “[Large image datasets: A pyrrhic win for computer vision?](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16923.pdf)” critiquing the culture of large-scale datasets in Computer Vision. Using ImageNet as a template, we investigated ethical transgressions including NSFW content, gender bias, and human cardinality.

Recently, we encountered a paper from the ImageNet curators titled “[A Study of Face Obfuscation in ImageNet](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.06191.pdf)” that has left us disappointed. By indulging in what appears to be a calculated and systematic erasure of the body of critique our work was a part of, the authors have sent out a wide range of wrong signals.

# Main Issues

### 1. The Curious Case of Face Obfuscation
In Section 4.3 of our paper, titled **"Differentially private obfuscation of the faces,"** we explicitly proposed harnessing techniques like DP-Blur to obfuscate identities. While privacy-preserving obfuscation is not our invention, it is reasonable to assume that a paper dedicated to this exact topic in ImageNet would cite the prior work that critiqued these very issues in the same dataset. Instead, the authors claim to have "discovered" the concern of people co-occurring with objects in images—a point we and others had already documented.

[![](/alignchronicles/assets/images/posts/imagenet-study-01.png)](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*kEeGOnP0BxupfUF3FE6OYw.png)

### 2. NSFW Analysis
The term NSFW appears 38 times in our paper. We dedicated an entire Appendix to misogynistic imagery hand-labeling and identified specific NSFW-dense classes like 'bikini' and 'miniskirt'. Disappointingly, the new paper claims to have discovered that "Bikini is likely to contain much more NSFW areas than the average" without acknowledgement of our prior quantitative census.

[![](/alignchronicles/assets/images/posts/imagenet-study-02.png)](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*E0OHMMOnygEhjxN-eAUL7w.png)

### 3. Human Co-occurrence Analysis
We dedicated Section B.3 of our paper to co-occurrence-based gender biases, specifically in musical instruments and dog breeds. The new paper mirrors these findings, stating that "super categories such as clothing and musical instrument frequently co-occur with people," yet treats this as a fresh observation.

# Proof of Awareness

To pre-empt the "it slipped through the cracks" defense:
* **Direct Contact:** We emailed the curators in August 2019 and April 2020 with no response.
* **Stanford HAI Seminar:** PL (the main visionary of ImageNet) was well aware of the work, having attended my Stanford-HAI seminar on this very topic in April 2020.
* **Prior Citations:** The same group cited us in another recent paper (*REVISE*), proving they were aware of our work, even if they chose to exclude it here.

# Concluding Thoughts: The Real Issues

### Erasure of Black-Women-Scholarship
Abeba Birhane’s central role in this work has been vital. Historically, Black women’s intellectual production has been systematically erased or undervalued. Seeing this behavior reinforced by esteemed scholars is unfortunate and highlights why initiatives like the *Cite Black Women* collective are necessary.

### The Horrors of Ghost Labor
To "clean up" the dataset, the authors resorted to the ghost labor markets of Amazon Mechanical Turk. They even gamified the task—workers lose "lives" if they make mistakes. This exploitation of labor to fix ethical debt is a step backward, not forward.

[![](/alignchronicles/assets/images/posts/imagenet-study-03.png)](https://cdn-images-1.medium.com/max/800/1*p0RkzmKXuJ8nRb4vEfuffQ.png)

This is not about citation-seeking; it is about calling out a **pattern of citation erasure** and highlighting the ethical shortcomings in "stop-gap" fixes that ignore the history of the critique that prompted them.
